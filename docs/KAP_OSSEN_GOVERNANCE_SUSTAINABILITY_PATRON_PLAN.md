## 1. Executive decision

Kap Ossen should be established as a durable family-development institution, not as a personality website, a campaign website or a collection of ambitious illustrations.

The recommended model is **Patron-led, charter-governed and professionally executed**:

- **Hon. Solomon Saigut Cherogony** serves as the proposed Patron and constitutional guardian of the family-development mission, subject to a recorded family resolution and his written acceptance.
- **Engineer Saigut Julius Kipkorir** remains the founder, originator and principal author of ARROR City Legacy and retains all associated rights unless he assigns them through a signed instrument.
- **ST-Firm** supplies engineering, technology, documentation and approved programme-management capability under written scopes and contracts.
- A **Kap Ossen Family Assembly** protects family legitimacy, heritage and reserved collective decisions.
- A **Family Development Council** converts the mission into governed programmes, budgets, partnerships and evidence.
- Independent committees protect finance, sustainability, heritage, privacy and future generations.
- A later **ARROR City/Gateway project vehicle** holds only the land, rights, finance and obligations expressly placed into it after title, planning and feasibility review.
- **Solomon Ops** remains a separate political campaign programme for Hon. Solomon Saigut Cherogony's 2027 Eldama Ravine parliamentary bid. It is never the governing system for Kap Ossen.

The Patron governs through a charter, meetings, counsel, reporting and defined powers. He does not govern by informal instruction, assumed family seniority or unrestricted access to land, money, intellectual property, personal data or campaign systems.

## 2. What this plan protects

This plan is designed to protect five things at the same time:

1. **The family** — its dignity, voluntary participation, history, privacy and ability to resolve disagreements.
2. **The Patron** — a respected office with a serious mandate, clear authority and protection from operational conflicts or unrecorded obligations.
3. **The founder** — Engineer Saigut Julius Kipkorir's authorship, technology work, brand rights and ARROR City originating vision.
4. **The assets** — land, money, archives, software, brands, data and future development rights.
5. **Future generations** — continuity beyond the 2027 election, current office-holders, market cycles and the lives of today's founders.

The institution succeeds only if it can survive:

- Solomon winning or not winning the 2027 election;
- a change of political party or public role;
- the absence, incapacity or retirement of a founder or Patron;
- a difficult SCOF market period;
- construction delays or an unfavourable feasibility result;
- family disagreement;
- a vendor, technology or financing failure; and
- a climate, water, energy or economic shock.

## 4. Governing doctrine

Kap Ossen should adopt ten constitutional principles.

1. **Heritage before publicity.** Family dignity and truth take priority over attention.
2. **Mandate before power.** Every office has a written source, term and limit.
3. **Evidence before claims.** Public statements require a source, owner and review status.
4. **Consent before exposure.** Family names, images, voices and records are used only with authority.
5. **Separation before scale.** Family, company, land, campaign, SCOF and personal resources remain distinct.
6. **Feasibility before construction.** Vision illustrations never substitute for survey, planning, environmental, market and finance work.
7. **Cash flow before speculation.** Productive revenue and reserves support development; a projected SCOF value does not fund the base case.
8. **Stewardship before extraction.** Land, water, soil, culture and community trust are treated as inherited capital.
9. **Succession before crisis.** Every critical role and asset has continuity arrangements.
10. **Future generations at the table.** Youth do not inherit only decisions; they participate in making them.

## 6. Office of the Patron

### 6.1 Purpose

The Patron is the senior institutional guardian of the Kap Ossen Family Development Programme. The office gives the family a dignified centre of gravity while preventing the mission from becoming informal, arbitrary or dependent on campaign machinery.

### 6.2 Proposed inaugural Patron

**Hon. Solomon Saigut Cherogony**, subject to:

- a recorded Family Assembly resolution;
- his written acceptance;
- approval of the Patron's mandate and conflict rules;
- verification of the public biography used on the website; and
- annual declarations covering political, business, family and project conflicts.

### 6.3 Core duties

The Patron should:

- guard the family-development mission, values and intergenerational purpose;
- convene and chair the Annual Kap Ossen Legacy Assembly;
- chair or nominate the chair for quarterly Development Council sessions under the charter;
- receive quarterly finance, sustainability, risk and delivery reports;
- request an extraordinary review where land, family dignity, financial integrity or public trust is threatened;
- open partnership conversations, while leaving contractual commitment to authorised signatories;
- represent the approved programme at ceremonial and public-interest engagements;
- encourage mediation before family disputes become destructive;
- mentor the next generation of family-development leaders;
- deliver an annual Legacy Address covering achievements, failures, finances, sustainability and the next year's priorities; and
- protect the separation between Kap Ossen family development and Solomon Ops political activity.

### 6.4 Positive powers

The charter may give the Patron authority to:

- call an ordinary or extraordinary Family Development Council meeting;
- require a written programme, risk or finance report;
- refer a decision for legal, ethical, land or sustainability review;
- recommend partnership and advisory appointments;
- sponsor an agenda item for Family Assembly consideration;
- issue a non-binding Patron's Guidance Note;
- grant ceremonial recognition under an approved honours policy;
- pause public representation in the Kap Ossen name for up to 14 days where an immediate truth, safety or dignity risk exists; and
- refer unresolved disputes to the Ethics, Conflict and Grievance Panel.

Any emergency pause must be recorded, reasoned, time-limited and reviewed by the Council or Family Assembly. It is not a permanent veto.

### 6.5 Reserved concurrence

Patron concurrence may be required, together with the other named authority, for:

- changes to the Kap Ossen mission or public family identity;
- appointment of a programme spokesperson;
- the annual public Legacy Address;
- use of the Kap Ossen name for a major external partnership;
- adoption of the annual programme plan and public impact report; and
- ceremonial opening of a completed family-development facility.

Concurrence is not sole authority. The corresponding family, land, company, financial, legal or IP authority must also sign.

### 6.6 Explicit limits

The Patron cannot, acting alone:

- sell, lease, charge, subdivide, pledge or transfer family or individual land;
- borrow money, guarantee a debt or commit family members financially;
- issue equity, profit rights, tokens or investment promises;
- transfer or license ARROR City Legacy, ST-Firm, SCOF or other intellectual property;
- appoint an ST-Firm director, employee or shareholder;
- access family-private, ST-Firm client, SCOF participant, KENAFF member or campaign data without a lawful role;
- convert the Family Embassy into a political campaign channel;
- use the Patron's office to compel votes, donations, endorsements or labour;
- approve his own related-party contract or reimbursement;
- override individual consent for a photograph, biography or testimony;
- make a verified public claim where the evidence register says “family account” or “pending”; or
- bind a future project company that has not authorised him as a signatory.

### 6.7 Term, renewal and succession

Recommended starting model:

- initial term: three years from formal appointment;
- annual performance and conflict review;
- renewable once by Family Assembly resolution;
- no automatic conversion of a political or public office into the family Patron's office;
- an Acting Patron selected under the charter for incapacity, conflict or temporary absence;
- a written succession protocol, not an assumed hereditary transfer;
- removal for serious misconduct, incapacity, persistent non-performance or material conflict through a defined supermajority and fair hearing; and
- an honourable Patron Emeritus title where service ends in good standing.

The family may choose a different term, but it must record the choice before launch.

### 6.8 Conflict and recusal

The Patron must disclose and recuse where a matter concerns:

- Solomon Ops or his own election campaign;
- his personal business, family household or financial interest;
- a contract with an associate;
- a public office he holds;
- an unverified public claim about his record; or
- a benefit available to him that is not equally governed for other participants.

During recusal, the Deputy Council Chair or an independent committee chair leads the item. The minutes record the declaration, absence from deliberation and decision-maker.

## 7. Family Assembly

### 7.1 Purpose

The Family Assembly is the source of family legitimacy for heritage, shared mission and specifically reserved collective matters. It does not absorb privately owned land, businesses, intellectual property or personal decisions.

### 7.2 Membership

The charter should define:

- recognised adult family members and branch representation;
- participation rights for spouses, guardians and young adults;
- non-voting expert or adviser attendance;
- a verified register with privacy controls;
- remote participation for family members outside Kenya; and
- a non-discrimination and respectful-conduct rule.

### 7.3 Reserved matters

The Assembly should approve:

- the Family Charter and major amendments;
- appointment, renewal or removal of the Patron;
- the Kap Ossen public mission and crest;
- creation or dissolution of the Family Development Council;
- use of shared family funds above an approved threshold;
- a transfer of a genuinely collectively owned family asset;
- the annual family-development plan and independently reviewed annual report;
- major public partnership use of the Kap Ossen name; and
- succession rules for family governance.

### 7.4 Voting model

Recommended:

- ordinary matters: simple majority of valid votes cast;
- charter, Patron and major identity matters: two-thirds majority;
- collectively owned land disposal or charging: the stricter threshold required by title, trust, succession, family agreement and law, never merely a website vote;
- quorum: representation from a defined majority of branches plus a minimum number of eligible members;
- private ownership: no assembly vote can dispose of an individual's asset without that owner's written lawful consent.

## 18. Environmental sustainability plan

### 18.1 Before design

- verify land ownership, boundaries, easements and current use;
- map slope, drainage, flood, erosion and geotechnical conditions;
- establish water availability and catchment constraints;
- map existing trees, habitats and productive soil;
- identify contamination or waste risks;
- study local climate and heat conditions;
- assess road access, traffic and public transport;
- document neighbouring communities and livelihoods; and
- establish a baseline before clearing or construction.

### 18.2 Design principles

- prioritise passive shade, daylight and ventilation;
- orient buildings to reduce heat and energy demand;
- cluster construction to retain productive or ecological land;
- use compact multi-level design only where ground, access, fire and market studies support it;
- integrate universal access from the first drawings;
- preserve and expand locally appropriate tree cover;
- separate clean rainwater, greywater and wastewater systems;
- design for repair, disassembly and material reuse;
- reserve safe walking, cycling and public-space routes;
- plan loading, forklifts and pedestrians as separate safety systems;
- minimise unnecessary basement excavation where groundwater, cost or carbon make it harmful; and
- require life-cycle cost and operational-energy analysis, not only an impressive rendering.

### 18.3 Energy

- efficiency-first building envelope and equipment;
- solar-readiness and phased renewable generation;
- battery/storage only after load, fire, replacement and recycling analysis;
- sub-metering by building and major tenant;
- efficient refrigeration and cold-chain systems;
- smart controls with manual fail-safe operation;
- backup power for life safety and critical operations;

## 21. Financial sustainability and treasury controls

- quarterly committee review;
- related-party declaration;
- restricted-fund tracking;
