## 4. The central development vision

Create a landmark, future-ready family development on the 30-acre property: a German-standard mixed-use market, mall, seasonal park, business centre, light-industrial/logistics hub and export platform that combines local identity with international operating discipline.

The project should be locally rooted, commercially practical, digitally managed and designed to use land in three dimensions: across the site, upward through multi-level structures and downward where safe and financially viable.

### Main components requested

1. **German-format food and household market**
   - Inspired by the clarity, efficiency, accessibility and operational discipline of retailers such as Lidl.
   - This is an inspiration reference only. The project must use its own name, brand, architecture and intellectual property unless a formal commercial partnership is obtained.
   - Features may include a clear customer journey, strong natural lighting, energy-efficient systems, simple signage, efficient loading and restocking, local produce sections and transparent pricing.

2. **Mixed-use family mall and business centre**
   - Flexible retail units and business rooms available to rent.
   - Shops, professional services, food, local brands, technology services and family enterprises.
   - Configurable unit sizes so small traders can start affordably and growing tenants can expand.

3. **Basement and structured parking**
   - Underground/basement parking is part of the ambition.
   - It is subject to geotechnical, groundwater, drainage, flood-risk, ventilation, fire-safety and financial feasibility studies.
   - Surface, shaded, accessible, bicycle, motorcycle, taxi, matatu and service-vehicle areas should also be considered.

4. **Seasonal local-products and cultural park**
   - A landscaped public destination for family gatherings, photography, markets, exhibitions and local cultural activity.
   - Strong December programme to capture holiday travel, homecoming, celebrations and seasonal spending.
   - Rotating seasonal themes rather than a space that is active only once a year.
   - Potential revenue from stall hire, events, photography locations, food, sponsorship, tickets where appropriate and temporary retail.

5. **Boutique street and natural public realm**
   - A walkable, attractive street with smaller shops, trees, shaded sitting areas, locally appropriate landscaping and memorable photography points.
   - The phrase “natural boutique” was used in the briefing; the preferred meaning should be confirmed during concept design.

6. **Industrial, logistics and export zone**
   - Warehousing, order fulfilment, aggregation, cold-chain options, loading docks and forklift-compatible circulation.
   - Designed to support local products and potential international trade.
   - Inspired operationally by large retail/logistics systems such as Amazon and Lidl, without copying their branding or suggesting affiliation.
   - Separate heavy-vehicle and customer circulation for safety.
   - Digital inventory, traceability, dispatch, tenant and loading management.

7. **Agricultural and local-producer platform**
   - Market access for local farmers and producers.
   - Product aggregation, quality checks, packaging, storage and potential export preparation.
   - Connection to the existing coffee, agriculture and SCOF pillars where appropriate.

8. **Technology and operations layer**
   - Digital tenant management, vacancy display, bookings, logistics scheduling, security operations, energy monitoring and performance dashboards.
   - “Powered by ST-Firm” or the intended organisation referred to orally as “Steve Farm”; exact brand name and authority must be confirmed.

## 10. Feasibility and approvals gate

Before architectural promises or investment invitations are published, complete the following:

1. Confirm the will, succession status, title, beneficiaries and authority to plan the full property.
2. Confirm parcel number, exact acreage, boundaries, encumbrances, easements and road reserve.
3. Commission cadastral, topographical, geotechnical, hydrological and utilities surveys.
4. Obtain a traffic and road-access study, particularly because the site is associated with a highway/weighbridge area.
5. Engage Baringo County planning authorities and a registered physical planner.
6. Determine existing land use, required change/extension of user and development permissions.
7. Engage KeNHA or the correct road authority for access-road/lane approval.
8. Complete the applicable NEMA environmental-impact process before development.
9. Prepare a market-demand, tenant, logistics and financial feasibility study.
10. Develop a concept master plan and test several phasing options.
11. Establish a family-approved project vehicle, governance rules and transparent decision rights.
12. Prepare capital cost ranges, funding options, risk register and development schedule.

Kenya’s Physical and Land Use Planning Act requires county development permission. The regulations require supporting ownership documents, location and scheme/building plans, and—in applicable cases—planning briefs and public notification. NEMA states that projects requiring environmental assessment should not proceed without the relevant EIA approval. Current KeNHA materials also identify approval procedures for access roads/lanes to premises.
